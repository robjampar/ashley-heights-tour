Working comfort allowances, not regulatory or universal minima:

- Prefer about 900 mm usable bed-side access and 1000 mm at a foot route; the sourced 800 mm benchmark is a check to interpret, not a luxury design target. Exclude bedside furniture from the usable strip.
- Start main shared routes around 1000–1200 mm, then test turns, doors and simultaneous use. A continuous 800 mm body path does not demonstrate a 1200 mm route.
- Begin wardrobe studies with a 1200 mm aisle, then add the actual open leaves/drawers and people. A closed-front aisle measurement does not prove simultaneous use.
- Start desks around 1600–1800 × 700 mm with roughly 900 mm chair operating space; any passing route behind an occupied chair needs its own check.
- For dining, state whether someone must pass behind a seated person. NKBA distinguishes 813 mm without passing from 1118 mm for walking behind, measured from the table/counter edge to the obstruction. Those are US recommendations; for this brief start relevant passing sides around 1200 mm and test the actual chairs. [NKBA guideline 8](https://media.nkba.org/uploads/2022/05/Kitchen-Planning-Guidelines.pdf).
- For kitchen work aisles, use the actual number of cooks: NKBA suggests 1067 mm for one or 1219 mm for multiple cooks. Still draw open appliances and standing people. [NKBA guideline 6](https://media.nkba.org/uploads/2022/05/Kitchen-Planning-Guidelines.pdf).

Bed-side reference: [CEUD checklist, printed p. 6](https://universaldesign.ie/uploads/publications/UDH-checklist-Accessible-PDF-May-2025.pdf). The other rounded allowances above are provisional Ashley Heights design targets. A complete accessible design requires more than these selected dimensions.
