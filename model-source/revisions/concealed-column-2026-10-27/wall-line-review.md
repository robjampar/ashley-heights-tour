# Response to the wall-line objection

The LP03 drawing put a new steel column next to an already modelled wall and then added a lining to hide it. It was premature to recommend that arrangement without first establishing whether the aligned wall could take the reaction. The proposal is not accepted and remains uninstalled. The original LP02 column stays in the live model.

## Measured geometry

At X5.2325/Y−9.67, a 100 × 200 mm plan probe is entirely contained within the footprints of all four of these actual current structural solids:

| Element | Role | Bounds across the wall, m |
|---|---|---|
| M-BLOCK-393739F | First-floor west recess inner leaf | X5.1825–5.2825 |
| M-BLOCK-C99B4D9 | Ground-floor west recess inner leaf | X5.1825–5.2825 |
| M-RC-51B8D91 | Proposed basement west retaining wall | X4.93–5.2825 |
| M-RC-EEBF948 | Proposed basement raft | X4.93–13.98 |

This is a geometric alignment check, not a bearing size or capacity calculation. These elements are marked as proposed new work in the register, even though they already appear in the house model.

A vertical ray through that point intersects the upper blockwork at Z2.8–5.35 and the lower blockwork at Z0–2.3026 and Z2.4426–2.6. The break at Z2.3026–2.4426 coincides with the local padstone zone. There is a further 200 mm gap between the lower wall's top at Z2.6 and the upper wall's base at Z2.8. The basement concrete extends from Z−3 to0 and meets the raft at Z−3. Consequently, the model must not yet be described as an uninterrupted, designed masonry bearing path through the floor.

The investigated column centre X5.40 was 167.5 mm inward of the block-leaf centre, to avoid intersecting the proposed retaining concrete. That choice generated the extra 222.5 mm wall lining. Keeping this separate support solely because masonry capacity had not been checked was an unnecessarily intrusive default, not proof that a column was required.

## Correct design decision

Investigate a beam bearing directly on an engineered padstone/spreader and the aligned inner leaf, including the floor-zone continuity, lower padstone interface, all coincident loads, eccentricity, wall restraint and the retaining-wall/raft actions. A padstone alone does not establish the wall's capacity. Do not assign a pad size or masonry strength from this footprint probe.

Only if that route fails should a locally reinforced pier or a steel column integrated into the wall be developed. The previous 150 mm SHS plus fire encasement cannot simply be declared to fit inside a 100 mm block leaf. Cavity insulation, moisture separation, fire protection and basement reinforcement must remain explicitly coordinated.

Moving the support onto the wall does **not** remove the transverse transfer beam: the main loft girder is at X8.32, about 3.09 m away. The LP03 cross-beam calculations also cannot be copied unchanged, because moving its support from X5.40 to X5.2325 changes the span and support movement assumptions. No replacement beam or bearing is selected in this review.

NHBC's [external masonry guidance](https://nhbcprod.blob.core.windows.net/shared/CC2981-NHBC-Standards-2024-aw-digital-version-lr-151223.pdf) requires concentrated-load bearing design and padstones/spreaders where the supporting masonry would otherwise be overstressed. This is the design principle being applied, not an assertion that this 100 mm wall passes. Current project standards, material strengths and survey evidence must inform the actual design.

Only review documentation changed. Native architecture, current structural members and their calculation records remain unchanged.
