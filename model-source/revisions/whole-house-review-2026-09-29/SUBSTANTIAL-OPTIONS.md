# 20 substantial alternatives

Local proposals for owner review. No accepted designs or publication changed.

## AH-003 · Replan arrival as a proper entrance sequence

Develop a coordinated arrival layout: preserve the shallow corner coat unit, place seated shoe-changing outside the through-route, and create one clear view from the front door into the original hall. Test a discreet internal glazed screen only where it improves zoning without narrowing the route.

Trade-off: A screen may interrupt the double-height openness; omit it if the route and sightline work better without it.

Check: Render arrival with doors open, coats in use and two people passing; compare the route to the current model.

## AH-008 · Turn the kitchen tall run into a pantry and appliance wall

Reconfigure the existing tall cabinetry into an integrated pantry, breakfast station and appliance bank. Keep the chosen island and main plumbing positions; use the tall run to take everyday clutter and preparation away from its social edge.

Trade-off: Pantry capacity competes with appliance and general storage; final cabinet allocation depends on actual equipment.

Check: Model doors and pull-outs open, check appliance ventilation and two-person use, and compare retained cabinet capacity.

## AH-012 · Create an integrated garden-dining bay

Compare a tailored banquette along a suitable solid wall with the current freestanding dining arrangement, integrating serving storage at one end and retaining a large everyday table. Keep garden glazing and door openings clear.

Trade-off: A banquette is less flexible and inner seats are harder to reach; do not proceed if no suitable uninterrupted wall exists.

Check: Test the current table size with people seated, chair pullback, serving and open garden doors before choosing the bay.

## AH-014 · Centre an eight-seat round table in the hexagonal dining bay

Compare a 1.70 m round oak pedestal table at the dining bay centre, with eight of the current upholstered chairs equally spaced around it. Re-centre the pendant and keep the lounge, windows, hall doors and drinks cabinet.

Trade-off: Eight permanent chairs occupy more of the room every day. The west/northwest chairs are close to the drinks cabinet; access behind occupied chairs and cabinet opening must be reviewed. This option replaces the previous six-seat extending brief for comparison only.

Check: Inspect matched native model photos and a dimensioned chair/pullback plan. Confirm door swings, cabinet access and circulation with chairs occupied and pulled out.

## AH-020 · Make the TV lounge acoustically separable

Compare a wide, pocketing glazed acoustic screen at the lounge transition, with coordinated ceiling absorption and concealed tracks. Keep it fully open for everyday family use and closed for films or calls.

Trade-off: Pocket walls, track depth and acoustic seals need space; ordinary decorative sliding glass will not provide the same isolation.

Check: Model the screen open and closed, check the parked leaves, thresholds and fixed TV sightlines; assess the actual assembly acoustically.

## AH-025 · Make the garage-gable alcove an intentional window retreat

Design a complete low seated retreat beyond the opening: fitted upholstered window seating, low integrated storage and concealed reading light shaped to the unchanged roof. Keep everyday wardrobe and bathroom circulation outside it.

Trade-off: It remains an optional duck-through destination; the low opening is not made full-height or represented as accessible.

Check: Render the approach and seated view using the real roof and opening; verify sitting headroom and window operation.

## AH-028 · Replan the ensuite as a coherent spa room

Compare a unified bathing and shower zone, a calmer double-vanity wall and a discreet WC sightline screen within the enlarged bathroom. Retain the bath window and wardrobe relationship; use a flush floor wherever feasible.

Trade-off: Moving drainage, screens or the shower can add construction work; a raised bath plinth is not assumed.

Check: Produce alternative occupied plans, wet/dry routes and matched 3D views; then check waste falls, cleaning access and floor support.

## AH-032 · Design the wardrobe as complete fitted dressing architecture

Redesign the internal joinery as coordinated full-height runs with dedicated long hanging, double hanging, folded storage and a concealed dressing perch. Keep the accepted room size, mirror and through-route, with no central island.

Trade-off: Storage allocation must fit actual clothing; full-height joinery can feel heavy unless openings, lighting and proportions are resolved.

Check: Compare measured hanging lengths and drawer capacities with the baseline, showing all fronts open and the route occupied.

## AH-035 · Replan the side bedroom and family-room boundary together

Study a further local transfer of space from the family approach into the bedroom, together with a revised ensuite entrance and storage position. Compare an approximately 450 mm northward partition study with the current layout, rather than just shrinking furniture.

Trade-off: The family room loses area and its approach changes. The 450 mm shift is a test dimension, not a verified recommendation; keep its main seating and external openings usable.

Check: Model both rooms together, measure occupied bed/wardrobe/door routes and reject any option that trades one pinch for another.

## AH-038 · Give the compact fourth bedroom a complete guest-room layout

Develop one coordinated bed-and-storage arrangement with full-height joinery on a suitable solid wall, an integrated occasional work/dressing surface and luggage space. Keep the accepted mattress and headboard; compare complete furniture layouts rather than extra shelves.

Trade-off: The room may not support every function simultaneously; the work surface may need to close away.

Check: Show sleeping, dressing and luggage-open states with all doors and windows operating in the actual shell.

## AH-047 · Make the loft a two-zone hobby and family room

Develop a permanent project/work zone with concealed ongoing-project storage and a separate soft family lounge, linked by a clear high-headroom route. Test sliding internal screens only where they park entirely out of the circulation.

Trade-off: Screens and storage can make the loft feel smaller; low eaves are not counted as normal standing space and removed eaves units are not reinstated.

Check: Render working and cleared states; map finished headroom, stair arrival and window access before approving the layout.

## AH-050 · Replan the gym and utility as a coordinated pair

Compare the current rooms with a compact stacked-laundry service wall that may release part of the gym/utility boundary for a better equipment zone. Retain both gate-facing treadmills and their rear safety areas; test any partition move only after fitting the full laundry brief.

Trade-off: The utility loses area and equipment/service costs may increase. Do not claim all activities can run together unless the occupied layout proves it.

Check: Use selected equipment dimensions, door swings, laundry loading and service clearances in a combined two-room model.

## AH-058 · Swap the games and lounge zones to open the basement route

Move the six-foot pool table into the west bay and move the sofa to the north wall of the east bay. Remove the separate west TV/console group and use the adjacent cinema for screen viewing; keep the bar and darts area. Test the whole zone swap with full cue envelopes.

Trade-off: It gives up the separate games-room TV nook. Cueing, occupied seating and access must all be checked; furniture fit alone is insufficient.

Check: Render the entire north room before/after, overlay the 1.525 m cue reservation and verify the circulation route around the stair and lounge.

## AH-062 · Rebuild the utility around stacked laundry and a drying cupboard

Compare a stacked laundry tower with a ventilated full-height drying cupboard and a continuous sorting/folding surface in the space released. Keep the existing sink service wall, east window and both door openings where possible.

Trade-off: Stacking reduces access to the upper machine for some users; stacking hardware, ventilation and maintenance access must match real products.

Check: Model appliance doors, basket handling and drying contents; compare storage and clear aisle widths with the current run.

## AH-066 · Replan garage storage around a protected pedestrian strip

Develop a complete garage layout with a clearly reserved pedestrian route to the utility, one consolidated perimeter storage wall and a genuinely retrievable bike zone. Keep two car spaces and the existing garage exterior/roof.

Trade-off: Some current cabinetry may have to move or shrink; larger real vehicles may rule out the desired bike arrangement.

Check: Use household vehicle envelopes, mirrors, boot and door travel, then show one person carrying shopping and one retrieving a bike.

## AH-068 · Design the pool terrace as one complete outdoor room

Develop one coordinated pool-side layout with integrated cover housing, a clear wet route, usable lounging positions and a distinct dry seating edge. Preserve the pool, approved lounger orientation and external house form.

Trade-off: Cover mechanisms and accessible service space consume deck area; additional level changes must not be hidden by attractive paving.

Check: Render the pool covered/open and the walking route occupied, using the actual site levels and all deck clearances.

## AH-070 · Turn the summer-house support rooms into a proper pool-changing suite

Study a dedicated shower/changing arrangement within the existing garden-building envelope, retaining the outside WC and relocating some tool storage to the workshop if necessary. Connect it to the existing changing bench and pool route.

Trade-off: New drainage and ventilation are required; tool storage is displaced and the existing envelope may limit the shower size.

Check: Draw the full garden-building plan, privacy sequence, drying space and drainage route; retain useful workshop storage and realistic wet clearances.

## AH-072 · Add a restrained covered poolside lounge

Compare a modest freestanding pergola/covered lounge in the existing garden layout, using warm timber and pale upholstery, positioned to preserve the approved loungers and pool circulation. Keep its scale subordinate to the house.

Trade-off: It changes the exterior and introduces wind, drainage and potential planning implications; it is an option for review, not assumed permitted work.

Check: Show model views from house, pool and neighbouring boundaries; compare sun/shade and access before choosing location, footprint or roof type.

## AH-076 · Compose the roof terrace as an outdoor living room

Develop a complete terrace plan with a sheltered lounge, a compact serving point and an unobstructed door-to-outlook route around the rooflights. Integrate lightweight screening only where sightline checks justify it.

Trade-off: Available seating area may shrink once realistic circulation, wind restraint, roof loading and drainage are accounted for.

Check: Render seated and standing views, rooflight access and occupied routes; coordinate the threshold, falls and structural limits separately.

## AH-079 · Design the workshop as a flexible creative studio

Develop a complete workshop arrangement with a continuous tool/storage wall, a clear central assembly zone and a cleaner design/project corner. Separate dusty work from clean storage through layout and local extraction rather than filling every wall indiscriminately.

Trade-off: The layout depends on the owner's tools and activities; extraction, power and noise provisions may be substantial.

Check: Show a large project on the centre table, tool access, material handling through the door and a cleared alternative state.
