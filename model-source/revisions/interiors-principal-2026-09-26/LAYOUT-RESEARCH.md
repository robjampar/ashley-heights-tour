# Principal suite: research before the next layout

Research date: 26 September 2026. The owner does not like the five floorplans and has asked us to study room planning before continuing. None is selected. This brief replaces the earlier recommendation to start with option 01 or 03.

Owner clarification: the bed, sofa and TV arrangement was the particular problem. The principles must be applied throughout the house; see `proposal/interiors/DESIGN-PRINCIPLES.md`. The next suite studies therefore compare furniture relationships before resolving the supporting rooms.

## What the next design must achieve

Keep a generous principal bedroom, comfortable TV viewing in bed, a proper sofa area, a walk-in/walk-through wardrobe, a useful but subordinate study, and a generous ensuite. The wardrobe should not dominate. The bed must not obstruct the entrance. Internal partitions may change. Investigate an ensuite at the former study end, but treat that as a hypothesis rather than a fixed instruction. One TV shared by bed and sofa was a possibility in the owner's brief, not an absolute requirement. Preserve the wider preference for realistic changes to the existing house.

The immediate job is spatial design. The warm oak, ivory and limestone palette can follow a successful layout; it cannot resolve circulation or proportion problems.

## What went wrong in the five studies

Inspection of `proposal/interiors/principal/floorplans.json` confirms that all five use exactly the same bed centre, orientation and footprint. Four also retain the same sofa centre. Moving the ensuite, wardrobe and desk around that fixed arrangement did not explore five substantially different bedroom experiences.

The audit in `floorplans/audit.json` confirms collision-free entrance sweeps, connectivity for a 500 mm body and two-dimensional TV sightlines. Those are narrow geometric checks, not evidence of a comfortable or beautiful suite. In particular:

- A 500 mm passage is not our comfort target. The check did not establish comfortable space for dressing, making the bed, opening drawers or passing another person.
- The TV audit checks screen off-axis angles, not the viewer's head rotation or vertical sightline. These are different quantities. It also does not establish a satisfying wall composition or check every occupied seating position.
- Large room areas were used as proxies for quality. A large wardrobe may contain little useful storage; a large bathroom may simply have long routes between its fittings.
- Several layouts distribute enclosed rooms along the east side. That can fragment the suite and give valuable windows to functions that do not benefit from them as much as the sitting or working areas. This is a design concern to test, not a measured daylight result.
- The nominal model footprint is 91.69 m², calculated from the union of the envelope and alcove polygons. That includes low-vault floor and internal partition footprints; it is not a surveyed net usable area or a full-height area. We should not treat every square metre as equally useful.

## Evidence and its application

The sources below include design guidance, product dimensions and built examples. They do not establish a single universally best layout. Our dimensional targets and proposed arrangements are explicitly distinguished from source recommendations. International guidance is used as a design reference, not as a statement of English regulatory compliance.

### 1. Draw the activity and its operating space together

The Irish Centre for Excellence in Universal Design's May 2025 checklist specifies 800 mm clear on both sides and at the end of a double bed, and a study zone with daylight and a minimum footprint of 1200 × 1500 mm including the chair. It distinguishes passage, turning and activity space. This is a much more useful reference than testing a small body against furniture alone. [CEUD checklist, printed pp. 6 and 9](https://universaldesign.ie/uploads/publications/UDH-checklist-Accessible-PDF-May-2025.pdf).

**Our application:** draw standing, sitting, drawer-opening and door-operating space as part of each furniture layout. Provide an unobstructed route to each side of the bed. Test the room while occupied, not just with closed furniture.

### 2. Establish the bedroom composition before dividing the remainder

McGee & Co.'s bedroom guide begins with the actual positions of doors, windows and built-ins; it treats the bed as an anchor and asks that seating fit the scale of the room. [Bedroom guide](https://www.mcgeeandco.com/pages/bedroom-guide).

**Our application:** compare different headboard walls and orientations, then fit the sofa, TV and route through the room. Judge the view from the entrance, bed and sofa before adding decorative detail. Give the bed a deliberate setting and avoid routing daily traffic through the sitting group.

### 3. Define zones without automatically creating another room

In the architects' description of MAS Apartment, large joinery panels manage privacy; the principal bed is centred to allow wardrobes along the wall behind it. This is a useful built example of storage and furniture defining space. It is not evidence that a floating bed will necessarily fit Ashley Heights. [AL7's project description and photographs, published by ArchDaily](https://www.archdaily.com/1084785/mas-apartment-al7-architecture-and-interiors).

**Our application:** test a headboard/joinery screen or a broad opening as alternatives to another full enclosure. A screen must have a purpose, retain daylight and leave generous routes around its ends. A wardrobe behind a bed needs two usable spaces, not a narrow leftover slot.

### 4. Openness and privacy can coexist

The official Rosewood London Grand Premier Suite sample plan places bedroom and living areas at the windowed perimeter and groups dressing and bathing inward. The bedroom joins the lounge through an offset opening. The operator describes a separate living room, dressing room and bathroom. These observations concern this sample plan, not every suite. [Official suite page](https://www.rosewoodhotels.com/en/london/accommodation/suites/grand-premier-suite) · [sample floorplan](https://images.rosewoodhotels.com/is/image/rwhg/ROSEWOOD-LONDON_-GRAND-PREMIER-SUITE_SAMPLE).

Aman Tokyo's official suite photographs show a bed and a distinct sitting area within a broader composition; the operator identifies a sunken living area. This is a useful example of zoning, not a proposal to introduce a level change at Ashley Heights. [Tokyo Suite](https://www.aman.com/hotels/aman-tokyo/accommodation/suite/tokyo-suite).

**Our application:** keep the sleeping and sitting spaces visually connected where useful, while allowing bathing and dressing to be closed off. Hotel precedents illustrate spatial relationships; they do not provide our domestic storage quantities or working-from-home requirements.

### 5. Design the wardrobe by contents and usable frontage

IKEA's UK PAX frame is 580 mm deep; doors and the chosen fittings must also be allowed for. This is a useful dimensional reference, not a specification to buy PAX. [PAX frame dimensions](https://www.ikea.com/gb/en/p/pax-wardrobe-frame-dark-grey-10458215/).

**Our application:** inventory short hanging, long hanging, folded clothing, shoes and drawers. Compare linear usable storage frontage and access, not only wardrobe floor area. As an initial design allowance, two 600 mm cabinet runs and a 1200 mm aisle require 2400 mm overall clear internal width. That arithmetic excludes wall thickness and must be adjusted for finished joinery, handles and open drawers. A central island earns its place only if the operating clearances work.

### 6. Plan bathing around daily use, privacy and services

NKBA recommends at least 762 mm clear in front of bathroom fixtures and a shower interior of at least 914 × 914 mm. These are US planning recommendations, not English regulations or the intended luxury specification. [NKBA bathroom planning guidelines, printed pp. 41 and 51](https://media.nkba.org/uploads/2022/05/Bath-Planning-Guidelines.pdf).

Duravit's planning guidance addresses water/drain connections, structure, sloping ceilings, and using partitions to give a toilet or shower privacy. It advises against placing the WC directly opposite the entrance. [Bathroom floor planning](https://www.duravit.com/en-en/planning-inspiration/magazine/ideas-design/bathroom-floor-plans/).

**Our application:** establish a dry arrival and vanity zone, screen the WC from bed and doorway views, and provide a sensible night route. Test two people using the room together. Check a north ensuite against actual drainage, floor build-up, headroom and structure before preferring it. Do not enlarge the bathroom just to consume the available north wing.

### 7. Separate three different TV questions

Samsung's immersive viewing recommendation is a screen occupying roughly 40° of the viewer's field of view, with an example of a 75-inch TV at about 2.3 m. That 40° is **not** permission to watch the screen 40° off its face and is not a neck-turn target. [Samsung viewing-distance guidance](https://www.samsung.com/ae/support/tv-audio-video/recommended-viewing-distance-for-your-tv/).

**Our application:** evaluate (1) screen size/distance, (2) the viewer's head direction and eye height, and (3) viewing angle relative to the screen. Test both bed occupants, the sofa ends, seated/reclined positions and possible obstructions. Compare a fixed shared screen, a swivelling screen and separate discreet screens only as design alternatives. Do not force the room into a diagonal furniture arrangement simply to retain one screen. A swivelling screen also needs a credible mounting, cable and swept-volume solution.

### 8. Give the desk a usable setting

HSE's display-screen guidance recommends arranging the desk and screen to avoid glare and bright reflections; directly facing windows can be problematic. [HSE DSE leaflet, printed p. 2](https://www.hse.gov.uk/pubns/indg36.pdf).

**Our application:** give the study natural light with a controllable relationship to the screen, room to pull back the chair, sockets and useful storage. Use a broad alcove or joinery screen if it meets the owner's working routine; do not assume a separate office is necessary, or that a narrow console counts as a workstation.

## Working dimensional targets for the next sketches

These are provisional **project design targets**, not a regulatory checklist, a complete accessible design, or proof that the existing options achieve them. Dimensions refer to actual finished obstructions, not room centre-lines or mattress size alone.

| Element | Initial target | What still needs checking |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Bed sides | 800–900 mm usable clearance | Bed frame, bedside units, access to controls, making the bed |
| Main route around bed foot | 1000 mm preferred | Increase if this is also the main route to dressing or bathroom |
| Principal circulation | About 1000–1200 mm where it serves several activities | Local door clear openings, turning and occupied furniture |
| Opposing wardrobe fronts | 1200 mm aisle to start | Actual open leaves/drawers, standing person and whether someone must pass |
| Hanging joinery | About 600–650 mm finished depth | Hangers, doors, mechanism and actual clothing inventory |
| Desk | Start around 1600–1800 × 700 mm | User's equipment; about 900 mm chair operating space, more for a passing route |
| Shower | Test around 1400 × 1000 mm or larger | Clear entry, drying area, splash containment, controls and headroom |
| Bathroom fixture approach | About 900 mm preferred in this brief | Product-specific access and two simultaneous users |
| TV | No default approval angle | Actual bed/sofa eye points, head direction, screen size, height and glare |

The ensuite area and wardrobe area should emerge from those requirements. There is no evidence here for imposing a universal ideal number of square metres.

## How the next round should be developed

1. **Record constraints first.** Separate known fixed features, movable partitions and unknowns. Show existing openings, door operation, full-height/low-headroom zones and possible service routes. Treat a wall's structural role as unknown unless established.
2. **Map routines.** Entry to bed; either bedside to WC at night; shower to dressing; one person getting ready while the other sleeps; working while the other watches TV; laundry and cleaning. Identify crossings and light/noise spill.
3. **Compare truly different organisations.** Test a conventional headboard wall with a connected lounge, a bedroom organised around a joinery screen, and a more distinct sitting bay. Each must change the actual experience of the main room, not just swap labels on adjoining rooms. Do not promise that all three fit before checking them.
4. **Draw furniture and activity spaces before final partitions.** Keep the desirable daylight and views for occupied spaces. Avoid isolated fragments of floor and excessive corridors.
5. **Review in plan and at eye level.** Show the entrance view, view from bed, view from sofa and night bathroom route. Use plain geometry first so materials do not distract from spatial weaknesses.
6. **Reject poor candidates before detailing.** Judge arrival, bedroom proportions, TV comfort, two-person operation, privacy, daylight, storage usefulness and extent of building changes. A collision test is a baseline, not a quality score.

The next candidate should feel like one coherent bedroom suite with supporting spaces. Moving the ensuite to the old study may help, but only if the resulting bedroom, sitting area and circulation become better. Research is complete for this first pass; the next design step is a small set of genuinely different spatial sketches, not further polishing of the five rejected plans.
