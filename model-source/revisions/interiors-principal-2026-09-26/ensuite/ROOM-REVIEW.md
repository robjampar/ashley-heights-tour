# Principal bathroom and walk-through wardrobe — development 01

Disposition: **furnished isolated suite ready for owner review**, for Proposed and Proposed Planning. The owner accepted the developed south-wall bedroom and requested both adjoining spaces. The accepted bedroom and retained shell are geometrically identical in this new study (476 mesh signatures per variant); the previously reserved floor is replaced with dressing oak and bathroom limestone. Neither whole-house baseline is overwritten.

The new partition sits beyond the east window, dividing the available northern wing into about 12.09 m² dressing and 12.00 m² bathroom. The sequence is bedroom → dry walk-through wardrobe → closable bathroom. All existing external openings remain.

| Review | Result and trade-off |
| --- | --- |
| Full-sized fittings | 1800 × 800 mm hollow oval bath; approximately 1430 × 1701 mm shower footprint; 1800 × 550 mm double vanity; north-facing wall-hung WC. Shapes are bespoke concept geometry using credible dimensions, not manufacturer installation CAD. |
| Door and movement | Existing 950 mm bedroom/dressing opening retained. New nominal 950 mm bathroom opening swings outward. Both full quarter-circle swings clear the storage. About 928 mm remains at a 90° leaf before final hardware. A 1 m dressing route starts after the bedroom door leaf, with rounded turn envelopes between the storage runs. The bathroom route is 900 mm. |
| Occupied use | Two separate 600 mm deep vanity standing zones leave 1040 mm to the WC screen; an independent 1 m strip is checked inside the room. A user at the basin nearest the bathroom door can briefly share the arrival area with someone entering. This is not described as two-person simultaneous use at every point. |
| Wet-room access | Shower nominal open edge is 900 mm, about 895 mm after glass. The connected approach is tested at 840 mm through its turn beside the vanity. Bath long-side approach is 800 mm; WC side entry between bath and screen is 915 mm, with a 750 mm front-use zone. The plan does not count the gap behind the bath as a route. |
| Privacy | Eighteen native entry-to-WC bowl/seated-body rays across the two variants hit the privacy return or open bathroom door; the threshold views hit the return. A 1.72 m limestone return screens the bowl, while preserving access around its north end. It is visual separation, not an enclosed/acoustically private WC. Bathroom windows have separate obscure privacy panes in the study; actual glazing treatment needs specification. |
| Storage | Six 650 mm deep tall bays over 3.9 m of frontage: two double-hanging, one long-hanging, two hanging with drawers, and one shoes/knits. Each has overhead storage. Four low drawers occupy 1.6 m below the east window, with their stone top at 620 mm against a 750 mm sill. Check inventory against the owner's actual clothes before final joinery specification. |
| Storage operation | Open west and window drawers are checked with separate 1 m passing strips. No island or loose dressing chair occupies the main route. The east window is retained above the drawers; final handle reach and sash operation need the surveyed window specification. |
| Detail | Native editable bath/basin cavities, waste and overflow, bath bridge, taps, separate controls, shower handset/hose, rainfall nozzles, linear drain slots, WC seat/flush plate/roll, towel warmer, stone reveals, mirror frames, illuminated joinery, hangers and clothing. Door leaves, handles and hinges are present. |
| Lighting and mirrors | Bathroom and dressing downlights, mirror sidelights, wardrobe lighting and bounded viewer fills. Three real planar reflections in the browser follow the camera. No mirrored camera images are baked into the native geometry. Mirror-to-mirror recursion is disabled to bound cost; these are interactive review lighting, not a photometric study. |
| Cleaning / products | Bath has approximately 190 mm nominal east and 141 mm nominal north bounding gaps; the east stone lining reduces the first to about 174 mm. Final product installation, cleaning and service access must be resolved. A full tank and occupants require a floor-loading check. Shower floor fall, glass size/support, threshold, splash containment and waterproof window reveals are not yet engineered. |
| Services | Bath relocation still needs survey, soil/waste routing and falls, extraction/duct discharge, floor structure and electrical design. The visible extraction grille represents the intended location, not proof of a viable duct. |
| Browser / human scale | Bathroom doorway, vanity, shower, bath, dressing arrival, wardrobe reverse, WC and cutaway are reviewed for both designs. Normal views retain the ceiling. Mobile width, design switching, camera persistence, plan load and error-free assets are checked. |

Research informing the arrangement: [Duravit's room-planning guidance](https://www.duravit.com/en-us/planning-inspiration/magazine/ideas-design/bathroom-floor-plans/) and the [1800 × 800 mm Luv bath envelope](https://www.duravit.com.au/bathtubs/700434000000000). These sources inform proportions and operation; they are not evidence of UK regulatory or planning approval.

Reproduction: run `preview_principal_ensuite.py -- compact` and `-- planning` through Blender, then `draw_principal_ensuite.py`, build the walkthrough and run `walkthrough/tests/principal-ensuite-browser.mjs`. Input files are the saved accepted bedroom studies, whose hashes are in each report. `measurements.json`, `browser-checks.json` and the two native reports are the evidence. The local `.blend` studies remain editable; source staging records their checksums.
