# Entrance and ground-floor halls — design development

The entry is a sequence: front door → coat/key storage or shoe bench → glass-lined garden passage → original hall → kitchen/formal rooms/stairs. The existing link, entrance angle, front doors, glazing and basement stair opening remain.

The measured shell ruled out furniture along the south side: the garage door and basement stair opening occupy that zone. A 1.96 m long, 650 mm deep oak cabinet sits beside the west return, facing east. A 1.20 m hanging section uses sliding fronts; the remaining bay holds keys, charging and bags, with shoe storage below. Its visible rear is finished joinery; this is a freestanding piece rather than a new partition. A 1.43 m shoe bench sits under the internal gym window, below its sill and clear of its door. Neither piece occupies the front-door sightline.

The attached passage keeps its garden views. Limestone continues through the arrival route, with one restrained plant at the south corner. The original hall keeps its stair, guard, radiator and door openings. Existing wall niches are simplified by removing decorative ribs, mouldings and ornaments, while retaining their actual liners and wall apertures. Plain stone shelves, small ceramics and modern bronze/opal lights replace the ornate fittings. A narrow full-height mirror sits beside the radiator; shallow relief art occupies the old coat-hook position above it.

The review follows the existing room-study controls and a measured plan; no new interaction pattern is needed. Cameras follow the arrival sequence, with a close detail view and a cutaway.
