# Formal lounge and dining — local developed study

Owner brief: six everyday dining places, extending to eight to ten. The accepted
warm oak, limestone, ivory and restrained bronze palette continues through both
designs. Original walls, hall apertures, fireplace position, garden openings,
front bay, radiator and kitchen hatch remain.

The selected crosswise table is 1800 × 1000 mm, extending to 2800 × 1000 mm.
Its centre is (7.92, 7.25). Two facing 2400 mm sofas and a slim oval stone table
form the conversation group. A curved chair and small table give the front bay
a separate reading place. A 1700 × 580 mm sideboard stores two 500 × 1000 mm
extension leaves and four 480 × 890 × 90 mm folded guest chairs. All folded
chair frames, cushions, pivots and individual padded racks are modelled.

Native tests verify all six full chair envelopes and that every stored-chair
and leaf part fits inside the sideboard. The dining chairs fit within a
500 × 540 mm plan envelope. Occupied chair plans use 600 mm depth outside the
table edge. Six, eight and ten seated arrangements pass the actual Navigation
checks with a 600 mm body to nine targets, including both entrances, garden
openings, kitchen hatch, both sofas and the front reading place. Chair pullouts
are checked separately against the shell and furniture. Pulling an end chair
back temporarily narrows passing space; no simultaneous passing claim is made.

The original hall leaves pivoted inside the wall thickness. They are replaced
by plain oak leaves in the same apertures with room-side hinges. Dining leaves
open 130 degrees west and 155 degrees east; drawing-room leaves open 90 degrees.
The four complete new leaves and six retained garden leaves pass 91-position
native sweeps, including handles, furnishings and the retained hall walls.
This corrects actual wall intersections rather than ignoring them in the test.

The isolated browser studies pass in both designs, including camera controls,
texture loading, detail parts, cutaway and mobile layout. A paired full-house
build is in progress; preservation and exact study/full-model comparisons remain
required before integration is declared complete. Nothing has been published.
