# Downstairs cloakroom — developed review

The retained internal shell is approximately 1.470 × 2.415 m. The doorway and WC service wall stay in place. The south window was already bricked up in both proposals; the obsolete pole, blue curtains and rings have been removed, and the inner face is ivory. The original reconstruction remains separate.

A 900 × 410 mm floating oak vanity replaces the 1.68 m cabinet run. Its open ceramic basin is 157 mm deep, with a wall mixer, pop-up waste, compact trap and a U-shaped upper drawer. The 300 mm drawer extension is checked at 31 positions against the fixed fittings. The service cut-out is deliberately wide enough to pass around the basin as the drawer moves, reducing usable upper-drawer storage. The lower drawer provides the broader compartment.

The WC stays on its previous service wall and has a concealed cistern, open bowl and seat, separate hinge details and two flush buttons. The mirror works in the native model and browser, with face lighting; towel, soap, paper and brush have usable places. The limestone floor and limited feature surface, ivory walls, oak and bronze continue the agreed palette.

Three circulation states check a 600 mm body with the door open, door closed and upper drawer extended. The retained doorway is approximately 679 mm wide. Basin and WC use share the floor area: this is a room for one person at a time. The original entrance leaf is tested through 91 angles against the new fittings; fixed fittings are not reduced to their nominal product sizes.

The recorded checks use evaluated triangle geometry for drawer and door intersections, and the production navigation rules for circulation. They do not establish an accessible layout or construction approval. Final ventilation, plumbing, door hardware and purchased fittings still need detailed selection against measured site dimensions. The shortened vanity explicitly reduces storage.
