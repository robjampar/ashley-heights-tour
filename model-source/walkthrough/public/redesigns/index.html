      PLANNING

      The question to resolve

      These are design judgements, not a planning decision or a guarantee of permission.

      What has been checked, and what still needs design

      All options need a measured and topographical survey, structural and fire design, drainage and services design, and assessment of neighbour windows, daylight and privacy. The loft layouts use low eaves; their whole floor areas are not full-height rooms.

      For E1 and E2, the new rear dormer has 2.10 m concept clear height. New loft-stair samples give 2.24 m at the centre and 1.98 m near the top-landing edges: applicability of the reduced loft headroom provision needs Building Control review. E3’s loft is workspace only.

      DESIGN BASIS

      A realistic planning conversation

      The local character assessment places Ashley Close in F05 Vine Avenue / Ashley Road, where detached spacing, setbacks and mature planting matter. That is why the retained-front options deserve serious consideration.

      The Residential Extensions SPD and adopted Local Plan are the design starting point. The emerging 2026 plan is a separate consideration. Retaining the front or lowering a ridge does not, by itself, establish acceptability.

      The owner confirms the site is outside the Green Belt. Council map screening on 25 September 2026 places the reconstructed plot outside The Vine conservation area and the nearby mapped Article 4 areas. This is a map check, not a formal land-charge search; heritage and flood checks remain open.

      TPO 19/1987 identifies a cherry (T4) and a tree group (G1) beside the site. Their surveyed positions and root protection must be checked against the pool, terraces, retaining walls and construction access. The GIS symbols do not show actual root protection areas.

      The four outside spaces plus double garage are the owner's brief; council parking guidance should be applied to the final scheme and location.
